## How Release Dates Get Announced: Reading the Signals

Studios rarely wake up one morning and announce a launch day out of nowhere. There's a recognizable pipeline, and once you know it, you can estimate how close a game is to shipping just by watching which stage it's in.

| Stage | What it looks like | What it tells players |
|---|---|---|
| Teaser | Cinematic reveal, no date | The game exists; likely years away |
| Store page live | Wishlist button appears | Development is real; marketing has begun |
| Gameplay reveal | Trailer showing HUD and combat | A playable slice exists |
| Window announced | "Coming 2026" | Roughly 6–12 months out |
| Firm date | Day, month, year | Usually 4–16 weeks out |
| Gold / launch trailer | Preload goes live | Days away |

Valor Mortis currently sits past the store-page stage, which is a genuinely encouraging sign. It means the project has cleared internal milestones serious enough to justify public marketing spend. The next major beat to watch for is either a gameplay deep-dive or a launch window.

### Avoiding the Most Common Traps

Two mistakes catch almost everyone. The first is trusting placeholder dates on retail listings — stores frequently insert a generic date purely to make a product page functional. The second is confusing a "coming soon" tag with a confirmed window. If the studio hasn't said it in their own voice, it isn't confirmed.
