If you are hunting the platinum, a solid **Valor Mortis trophy guide** is the difference between a clean 100% run and hours of backtracking through areas you already cleared. Trophy lists in action-driven games reward planning: a couple of hours of preparation up front can delete an entire replay from your schedule. This **Valor Mortis trophy guide** breaks the work into phases, flags the categories that cause the most headaches, and shows you how to keep every requirement on track from the first hour to the final cleanup.

Below you will find a roadmap you can adapt to whatever your list throws at you, plus tables for missables, collectibles, and difficulty-related trophies. Nothing here depends on glitches or exploits — just efficient routing, disciplined save management, and a clear order of operations.

## Start by Reading the Shape of the Trophy List

Before you press start on a serious run, open the trophy or achievement list and sort it mentally into buckets. Most modern action games use the same skeleton: a large block of story progression trophies, a handful of combat mastery challenges, a collectible set, optional side content, and one or two difficulty or New Game Plus requirements.

Understanding which bucket each trophy belongs to tells you *when* to chase it. Story trophies take care of themselves. Collectibles and side content are the ones that punish you for being careless.

| Trophy Category | What It Usually Asks | Missable Risk | When to Tackle It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story progression | Reach chapters, defeat major encounters | None | Play naturally |
| Combat mastery | Kill counts, parries, no-damage fights | Low | Spread across the whole run |
| Collectibles | Lore items, relics, caches, audio logs | Medium to High | Track from the first hour |
| Side quests | Optional NPC chains with branching outcomes | High | Before every point of no return |
| Difficulty / NG+ | Finish on a harder setting or carry progress over | Low | Decide before you start |
| Systemic misc | Crafting, upgrades, hidden interactions | Medium | Cleanup phase |

The single most valuable habit is a written checklist. Keep a note on your phone or second screen and tick items off as you go. Memory is a terrible tracker for a 30-hour game.

## The Phased Roadmap: How to Route a Platinum Run

Treat the platinum as a project with four phases rather than one long grind. This structure works whether the list is short or packed with optional objectives, and it keeps you from wasting a playthrough on content you could have finished the first time.

| Phase | Primary Goal | Watch Out For | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Blind story run | Learn systems, finish the campaign | Missable side quests closing early | Skipping dialogue that opens quests |
| 2. Side content sweep | Clear optional quests, faction tasks, minigames | Locked areas after story beats | Rushing to the final boss |
| 3. Difficulty run | Finish on the hardest setting or NG+ | Carrying over the wrong save | Starting without upgrades transferred |
| 4. Cleanup | Collectibles, kill counts, hidden trophies | Duplicate tracking across saves | Assuming auto-save covers you |

**Phase 1 — the blind run.** Play the campaign on a comfortable difficulty. Explore side rooms, pick up anything glowing, and talk to every named NPC twice. You are not optimizing yet; you are mapping the game.

**Phase 2 — the sweep.** Before the final act, finish every optional thread you can. Most games close doors here permanently. This is where a **Valor Mortis trophy guide** earns its keep, because the order you complete side content often determines whether a trophy fires at all.

**Phase 3 — the hard run.** If a difficulty trophy exists, this is it. Do it after you know enemy patterns and after you have learned which upgrades trivialize certain fights. Player experience consistently shows that a second run on hard goes faster than a first run on normal, because you already know the layouts.

**Phase 4 — cleanup.** Mop up kill counts, hidden interactions, and stragglers. This phase is short if you tracked properly and painful if you did not.

## Missables, Choices, and the Save Hygiene That Saves You

Missable trophies are the number one reason people abandon a platinum. They usually come in three flavors: quests that expire when a chapter ends, branching choices that lock one reward, and areas that seal shut after a story event.

| Risk Type | Why It Bites | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter-gated quests | NPC disappears after the next story beat | Finish side content before every major mission |
| Branching choices | One path awards the trophy, the other does not | Keep a manual save before the decision |
| Sealed areas | Collectible becomes unreachable | Clear the region before advancing the plot |
| Timed events | Window closes after a set number of days | Prioritize them early in each act |
| Companion outcomes | Ending depends on accumulated actions | Track relationship or reputation flags |

A practical routine: before every story mission that feels important, make a **manual save in a dedicated slot**, then upload it to cloud storage if your platform supports it. Rotate three or four slots so you always have a fallback. Community reports repeatedly confirm that auto-saves overwrite too quickly to be a safety net.

If a choice splits into two trophies, do not replay the whole game for the second one. Finish the run, then reload the pre-choice save and take the other branch. That single habit can cut ten hours off a completion.

## Collectibles, Combat Challenges, and Efficient Cleanup

Collectibles are the least glamorous part of any **Valor Mortis trophy guide** and the most common source of a stalled platinum. The trick is to treat them as a routing problem, not a hunting problem.

| Collectible Type | Where They Hide | Best Tracking Method | Reset Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lore documents | Desks, shelves, camps, ruins | Region checklist with counts | None |
| Relics and artifacts | Off-path ledges, hidden rooms | Screenshot each pickup screen | Low |
| Caches and supplies | Behind breakables, under stairs | Clear a region in one pass | Medium |
| Audio logs | Boss arenas, story rooms | Chapter-by-chapter sweep | High if you skip rooms |
| Map fragments | Optional dungeons | Cross-reference the world map | None |

Clear collectibles **region by region**, not trophy by trophy. Wandering the whole map for one missing item is the classic time sink. When you finish an area, confirm its counter is complete before you move on — most games expose a per-region tally in the map or journal.

For combat challenges, stack requirements whenever possible. If one trophy wants a hundred kills with a specific weapon and another wants finishers, use that weapon *and* finish enemies with it. Overlapping objectives turn two grinds into one.

## Common Mistakes That Cost You the Platinum

- **Skipping the pre-run research.** Ten minutes with the trophy list prevents most wasted hours.
- **Trusting one save file.** Corruption, overwrites, and bad branches all end the same way.
- **Leaving collectibles for last.** Without region tracking, the final hunt becomes guesswork.
- **Ignoring kill-count trophies until the end.** You will end up farming respawning enemies for an hour.
- **Starting the hard run too early.** Difficulty trophies are easier once you understand enemy tells and upgrade paths.
- **Assuming the list is identical across platforms.** Trophy and achievement requirements can differ, so verify on your own system.
- **Not reading trophy descriptions carefully.** "Defeat X without taking damage" and "Defeat X without dying" are very different asks.

The good news is that every one of these is preventable. A **Valor Mortis trophy guide** is only useful if you actually follow its order of operations, so commit to the roadmap early and resist the urge to improvise halfway through.

## Frequently Asked Questions About This Valor Mortis Trophy Guide

### How long does the platinum take?

It depends heavily on whether the list includes a difficulty run and a large collectible set. Games with both typically land in the long category; lists without a second playthrough requirement are dramatically shorter. Track your own progress against the phase table above rather than chasing someone else's number.

### Can I get every trophy in a single playthrough?

Sometimes, but rarely without care. If the list contains mutually exclusive choices or a higher-difficulty requirement, you will need at least a partial second run or a reloaded save. Check for choice-based trophies before you lock in major decisions.

### Are any trophies permanently missable?

Yes — chapter-gated quests and sealed regions are the usual culprits. Keep manual saves before story milestones and finish optional content as early as the game allows. If you have already passed a point of no return, reload an older save rather than restarting the entire campaign.

### Do collectibles carry over between playthroughs?

In most games of this style, collectible progress is tied to the save file rather than the profile, so a fresh run resets your counters. New Game Plus usually preserves them. Confirm this in your own menu before starting a second run, because it changes how you plan cleanup.

Work through the phases in order, keep your saves clean, and check off requirements as you go. Do that, and the platinum stops being a grind and becomes a checklist you simply finish.
